# FORGE BUILD PACKET: DAVID WEEKLEY HOMES CONCEPT REDESIGN

## Role

Build a complete FORGE-native redesign concept for David Weekley Homes. This is an independent design study based on public company information. It is not an official David Weekley Homes website and must not imply endorsement.

## Current-state audit

The existing public website leads with a market selector and contains strong trust material, home-search inventory, homebuying guidance, company history, and customer reviews. Its principal weakness is not missing information. It is that discovery, brand proof, education, inventory, and conversion read as separate stacked modules. The redesign must keep those functions while turning them into one continuous buyer journey.

## Controlling thesis

Do not make the visitor browse a builder. Let them move from place, to community, to home, to threshold, to personal choice, to a scheduled visit.

## Memory test

The visitor remembers crossing from a Florida landscape into a home and seeing the same horizon continue through the glass.

## Primary signature moment

The protected signature slice is `community` through `threshold`. The neighborhood establishes the water and landscape line. The residence model enters that same frame. The camera advances through the architectural opening, and the interior media inherits the horizon and light direction. Forward scroll enters the home. Reverse scroll reconstructs the exterior.

## Creative DNA

- Human, optimistic, established, and regionally specific
- Premium without luxury clichés
- Architectural photography supported by restrained spatial depth
- Warm white, navy, sunlit coral, charcoal, and natural oak
- Editorial clarity before spectacle
- One strong spatial threshold instead of effects in every chapter

## Hierarchy contract

1. Find a home by place.
2. Understand what life there feels like.
3. Experience the home as a spatial sequence.
4. Understand Design, Choice, and Service.
5. See credible homeowner proof.
6. Schedule a personal tour.

## Asset reality

- Use only assets already registered in the repository.
- Use the prepared pavilion GLB as the architectural spatial anchor.
- Use the checked-in low model for constrained devices.
- Use the existing approved image URLs already present in the Forge asset manifest.
- Do not scrape, copy, or hotlink proprietary David Weekley photography.
- Do not fabricate floor plans, pricing, promotions, awards, availability, or testimonials.

## DOM and WebGL contract

DOM owns navigation, headings, body copy, brand proof, buttons, market-search actions, legal language, and the tour form. The persistent FORGE canvas owns camera, pavilion geometry, lighting, depth, and the threshold passage. Cinematic media owns the photographic environment. All systems derive from the same normalized progress.

## Signature Slice Gate

Do not treat the full experience as approved unless:

- the community to threshold sequence reads as one place;
- the pavilion model is visible and correctly framed;
- forward scroll enters and reverse scroll exits without a discontinuity;
- copy never competes with the architectural opening;
- mobile preserves the threshold idea;
- reduced motion preserves the sequence with a stable exterior and interior pair;
- the required model and media are preloaded before the slice begins.

## Acceptance contract

- One persistent FORGE canvas
- No second animation clock
- No invented asset paths
- Eight complete scenes with intentional contrast
- Exactly one spectacle peak
- Functional semantic tour conversion
- Useful no-JavaScript and WebGL fallback content
- Deliberate desktop and mobile compositions
- Reverse traversal reconstructs valid states
- No debug or Studio interface in production
- No claim that the concept is an official David Weekley Homes property

## Reference deconstruction

The current site is used only to understand information architecture and conversion requirements. The redesign does not copy its exact layout, typography, palette, imagery, or component styling. Transferable requirements are market-first discovery, trust proof, homebuying education, broad geographic coverage, and prominent contact paths.
