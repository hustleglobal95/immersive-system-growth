# Forge Structure Engine

Forge's Structure Engine defines website architecture **above the scene layer**. Scenes describe cinematic moments. Structure describes why those moments exist, what user question they answer, what evidence belongs there, how navigation should work, and when conversion asks should occur.

## Why this exists

A beautiful sequence of scenes is not automatically a strong website. Premium sites still need hierarchy, orientation, evidence, decision support, conversion logic, semantic navigation and a coherent mobile reading order.

The Structure Engine therefore separates:

1. **Site archetype** — what kind of website is being made.
2. **Commercial tier** — how much structural depth the engagement justifies.
3. **Page architecture** — the sitemap and purpose of each page.
4. **Section grammar** — hero, proof, product, experience, specs, FAQ, conversion and other semantic roles.
5. **Section intent** — the user question, evidence, media, motion energy and density for each chapter.
6. **Structural audit** — baseline sequencing, proof and conversion checks before visual polish.

## Archetypes

- Brand Flagship
- Product Launch
- Property Development
- Hospitality Destination
- Portfolio / Studio
- SaaS / Digital Product
- Editorial Commerce
- Campaign Story

These are structural systems, not visual themes. Art direction, typography, media treatment and motion language remain independent.

## Tiers

### Cinematic
Disciplined premium structure. A single primary narrative, limited depth and one clear conversion path. Target: roughly 5–7 cinematic scenes.

### Immersive
Adds experiential chapters, richer media and stronger environmental storytelling. Target: roughly 7–10 scenes.

### Signature
Adds technical proof, decision-support content, comparisons, social proof or specification depth where relevant. Target: roughly 9–13 scenes.

### Flagship
Adds bespoke editorial, team, cultural or campaign layers while preserving hierarchy. Target: roughly 11–16 scenes.

The tier system is intentionally **not** page-count pricing. Higher tiers add decision depth, evidence, interaction and narrative resolution.

## Structural rules

- The hero communicates the primary promise before visual effects require interpretation.
- Every major chapter answers a user question or provides evidence.
- Proof appears before high-friction conversion asks.
- Navigation remains understandable without motion or WebGL.
- Mobile may simplify motion but preserves information and conversion order.
- Avoid repetitive density and energy across consecutive sections unless repetition is deliberate.
- Use one primary CTA language per page.
- Cinematic moments are structural when they reveal meaning; otherwise they are decoration.

## Using the planner

Open `/structure` in the Forge app. Choose a project archetype and tier. The planner displays:

- recommended pages
- navigation mode
- section sequence
- each section's purpose
- the visitor question it answers
- expected evidence
- preferred media
- suitable motion treatment
- density and energy
- conversion weight
- structural audit score and notes

## Relationship to existing Forge systems

The Structure Engine does not replace:

- experience templates
- motion archetypes
- camera direction
- interaction graphs
- Type Vault
- visual systems

It coordinates them. Structure decides **what the website needs to communicate and in what order**. The cinematic systems decide **how that communication moves and feels**.

## Production workflow

1. Define project type and commercial tier.
2. Generate/select a structure plan.
3. Remove sections that do not have real evidence or a business purpose.
4. Map structural sections into Forge scenes and normal-flow DOM sections as appropriate.
5. Choose typography and art direction.
6. Assign media and 3D assets.
7. Apply camera/motion archetypes.
8. Add interaction only where it improves comprehension, exploration or conversion.
9. Run structural and technical audits.
10. QA desktop and mobile as complete websites, not just isolated cinematic frames.
